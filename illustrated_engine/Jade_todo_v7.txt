Jade, V6.2 rendering infrastructure is now considered stable.
Do not redesign the renderer. Do not return to Manim. Do not add AI video generation. Do not chase style-continuity scores. Do not add uncontrolled sub-agents. Do not increase context unnecessarily.
The next objective is V7 — Editorial Intelligence & Anti-Template.
The goal is:
Make the generated video feel like a human-edited mini-documentary rather than a sequence of generated slides.
P0 — 1. Eliminate narration-card dependency
The current blurred-background + centered-narration-card pattern is still overused.
Classify visuals as:
	•	EVIDENCE — diagram, transformation, comparison, map, timeline, process, scale, quantitative graphic, cutaway, cause/effect.
	•	CINEMATIC — meaningful subject-specific illustration.
	•	BREATHING — restrained typography/atmosphere.
Generic blurred-background narration cards must become an exception, not a normal shot type.
Initial gates:
	•	EVIDENCE + CINEMATIC ≥ 80% of narration duration
	•	BREATHING ≤ 20%
	•	Generic blur-card ≤ 10%
A subtitle over a blurred picture does not count as visual evidence.
 
⸻
 
P0 — 2. Make the first 2 seconds a real hook
The opening must establish:
SURPRISE / QUESTION / CONTRADICTION / SCALE / IMPOSSIBLE FACT
plus immediate visual proof.
Do not spend 5–8 seconds introducing the subject before revealing the curiosity gap.
The planner must explicitly generate:
	•	hook_claim
	•	hook_visual
	•	curiosity_gap
	•	payoff
The opening must make sense even with audio muted.
 
⸻
 
P0 — 3. Remove persistent large titles
Large title/header is allowed during the opening.
After approximately 2 seconds, remove the persistent large episode title.
Use only subtle section markers/branding when genuinely useful.
Do not let every video look like the same branded presentation template.
 
⸻
 
P1 — 4. Introduce conceptual continuity chains
Stop treating every shot as an independent slide.
Create persistent conceptual objects such as:
	•	Black hole: blackhole + far_clock + near_clock
	•	Aircraft: 747_wing + airflow + pressure_field
	•	Mitochondria: cell + mitochondrion + bacterial_ancestor
Objects should be able to:
	•	move
	•	zoom
	•	split
	•	transform
	•	connect
	•	compare
	•	become annotated
	•	change state
Prefer:
same object → transformation → consequence
over:
new image → narration → new image
 
⸻
 
P1 — 5. Add Information Transformation Score
For every major beat calculate:
information_transformation
A visual change counts only if it communicates a new:
	•	relationship
	•	state
	•	comparison
	•	scale
	•	process
	•	causal consequence
Camera movement alone does NOT count.
Number pops alone do NOT count.
Decorative motion does NOT count.
Repeated imagery with changed narration should score near zero.
 
⸻
 
P1 — 6. Add Visual Redundancy detection
Calculate redundancy over rolling 5-second windows.
Detect:
	•	repeated background
	•	repeated composition
	•	repeated camera state
	•	repeated diagram
	•	repeated layout
	•	repeated blur narration card
Flag periods where narration changes but meaningful visual evidence does not.
 
⸻
 
P1 — 7. Let the story determine the visual grammar
Do NOT force every video into:
hero → narration card → diagram → narration card → diagram
The planner must select a structure appropriate to the story.
Examples:
Aircraft
impossible load → wing → airflow → pressure → downward deflection → lift → consequence
Mitochondria
modern cell → mitochondrion → strange DNA → ancient bacterium → engulfment → retained DNA → modern payoff
Black hole
two clocks → move one closer → clocks diverge → spacetime explanation → distant observer perspective → horizon → payoff
The sequence must emerge from the explanation, not from a fixed shot template.
 
⸻
 
P0 — 8. Upgrade factual QA to semantic verification
Current claim verification is not sufficient.
Verify:
claim + exact wording + qualification + observer/perspective + number + object + timeframe
Examples:
Do not allow:
“A 747 weighs 400 tonnes”
without defining the relevant variant/context.
Do not allow:
“Time runs slower near a black hole”
when observer perspective is scientifically important.
Do not allow superlatives such as:
oldest / largest / nearest / first
without explicit definition, verification date and authoritative source.
A source supporting the general concept is insufficient if the exact narration overstates it.
 
⸻
 
P0 — 9. Make the ending a payoff
The final 3–5 seconds must resolve the curiosity established at the beginning.
Do not introduce an unrelated new fact in the final beat.
Require:
hook_question → story → answer/payoff
The final visual should be one of the strongest explanatory or emotional visuals in the video.
Do not end with generic imagery merely because the narration has finished.
 
⸻
 
P0 — 10. Add an Anti-Template system
Maintain a rolling structural signature of recent videos.
Track:
	•	opening structure
	•	shot-role sequence
	•	layout sequence
	•	blur-card ratio
	•	diagram ratio
	•	cinematic ratio
	•	average shot duration
	•	transition pattern
	•	title behavior
	•	visual-mode sequence
Compare every new video against recent videos.
Do NOT randomly vary these parameters.
The story should determine the structure.
The goal is:
Consistent brand, variable storytelling.
Keep consistent:
	•	typography family
	•	overall visual quality
	•	color philosophy
	•	narration quality
	•	audio quality
	•	brand identity
Vary:
	•	storytelling structure
	•	visual grammar
	•	shot sequencing
	•	information representation
	•	animation behavior
	•	transitions
	•	opening mechanism
	•	ending/payoff
 
⸻
 
P0 — 11. Upgrade the Human Editor Test
Add these questions:
	1.	Would I mistake this for an automated slideshow?
	2.	Does every narration beat have a meaningful visual?
	3.	Does the visual actually explain the sentence?
	4.	Is the opening compelling within 2 seconds?
	5.	Does the video contain genuine visual transformation?
	6.	Does the story maintain conceptual continuity?
	7.	Does any shot feel like filler?
	8.	Does the ending pay off the opening?
	9.	Could this exact visual structure be reused for another topic by merely changing the nouns?
	10.	Does this feel like a human editor made deliberate visual decisions?
A video should not be CAN_PUBLISH if it fails #1, #2, #4, #8 or #9.
 
⸻
 
P1 — 12. Keep all V6.2 technical foundations
Do NOT remove or weaken:
	•	motion smoothness QA
	•	continuous audio bed
	•	audio completion
	•	overlay compliance
	•	caption safe-zone QA
	•	frame distinctness
	•	CAS
	•	subject correctness
	•	factual verification
	•	phone QA
These are now foundation infrastructure.
 
⸻
 
V7 TEST PLAN
Do not test with three more nearly identical science explainers.
Create three deliberately different stories:
A — Science/process
Example: Why Your Phone Gets Hot While Charging
Visual grammar: cause → process → heat → consequence.
B — History
Example: How One Tiny Mistake Sank the Titanic
Visual grammar: chronology → reconstruction → causal chain → consequence.
C — Geography/natural phenomenon
Example: Why the Sahara Is Getting Greener
Visual grammar: map → time → transformation → comparison.
The purpose is to prove that V7 is a general storytelling engine, not merely a better science-diagram engine.
 
⸻
 
V7 REPORT REQUIREMENTS
Do not report only:
Technical / Visual / Editorial / CAN_PUBLISH
Also report:
	•	Hook score
	•	Evidence coverage %
	•	Cinematic coverage %
	•	Breathing %
	•	Generic blur %
	•	Information Transformation Score
	•	Visual Redundancy Score
	•	Conceptual Continuity Score
	•	Structural Diversity / Anti-Template Score
	•	Semantic Factual QA
	•	Ending Payoff Score
	•	Human Editor Score
	•	Top 5 genuine human-editor problems
The Top 5 human-editor problems must be based on actual viewing, not simply the five easiest automated QA failures.
Final principle
Do not optimize for internal scores.
Optimize for:
Would a real person stop scrolling? Would they understand the idea? Would the visuals make the explanation easier to remember? Would they watch to the end? Would they feel a human deliberately edited this video? Would the next video feel meaningfully different rather than template-generated?
V6.2 is the technical foundation.
V7 is about making the editor smarter.
